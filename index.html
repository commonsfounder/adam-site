Problem01

AI agents can already book, buy and write for you. But they only work where life already runs on software, so they have helped the people whose days live in inboxes, Slack and flight portals. Everyone else is left asking what it would do for them. Their life is not an app. It is the house, the family dinner, the music in the background: the place where we play, create, and stay connected.

You can’t want help you haven’t realised you could have, and most people won’t open an app and explain themselves every time. Help has to already be in the room, understanding you without being told twice. And although there are already devices in the home that listen, they just were not built for you.

Their ads come first

Many come from companies that earn from advertising, so what they learn about you is worth money to them.

Their ecosystem comes first

The more private ones lock you into one brand, and anyone in the house without the right phone gets a worse experience.

Their cameras come first

Cameras are now standard in the smart home, with the footage sent to someone else’s cloud.

Their product comes before your life

Wearable assistants ask for a new habit and something new to charge. Shopping agents take over tasks many people enjoy.

    How it works03

    Adam does not rely on sound alone.

    01

    Sense

    Four microphones, a 24 GHz presence radar, and temperature, humidity and light sensors. A low-power radio keeps listening for smoke and CO alarms even while the processor sleeps.

    02

    Combine

    A sound means little by itself. Adam weighs it against presence, the time of day, who is home, your routine and what it has learned about your household.

    03

    Act

    Then it does the right thing for that moment, and asks first before it sends, books or pays.

    A fall

    An impact sound, then no movement. Adam alerts your family, or the emergency services.

    A baby crying

    The sound of crying, movement in the room and who is home. Adam tells the right grown-up in the night.

    A break-in

    Glass breaking, then a window opening while nobody is home. Adam can act before you ask.

    A warm home

    Your routine, your phone’s location and your thermostat. The house is comfortable when you arrive.

    In a simulated 60-day house test, adding sensors to sound raised break-in detection from 44% to 97% and kitchen fire detection from 23% to 98%, and cut false alerts by about two thirds.

    We tested 15 everyday household sounds from FSD50K, a public research collection, on clips the model never trained on. Homes are noisy and echoey, so we added noise, echo and very short clips, and held both systems to the same strict limit of 1 false alarm in 100. Averaged over those hard conditions, ours catches about a quarter more real events than Apple’s, 54% against 43%. It has learned from public recordings only so far, and real homes are what will improve it.
