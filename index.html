    Adam listens for the moments that matter.

    1. A fall

      Adam asks if they are okay. With no answer in about 30 seconds it tells the family, and if nobody responds, it calls 999. Saying “I’m fine” stops it at any point.

    2. The smoke or CO alarm

      It tells whoever is home, wherever they are in the house, day or night.

    3. A baby crying

      It lets the parent on duty know, even with the telly on downstairs.

    4. A knock at the door

      It tells you, even when you are out in the garden.

    5. Glass breaking

      It alerts you straight away.

    It knows who is in which room, so the message reaches the right person.

    It helps with the everyday too. Reminders and routines, the family calendar, a briefing each morning, messages and emails, and orders and bookings with your OK. It remembers what you tell it, answers out loud, and keeps an eye on prices and deliveries.

    Kit day.

    6:50 am
    1. 6:50 am, Kit day. Adam remembers what the family has told it, and says it at the moment it matters. Adam: “Today is PE. Theo’s kit is by the dryer.”
    2. 11:30 am, A knock at the door. Dan is in the garden and cannot hear the door. Adam hears the knock and tells him. Adam: “Someone is knocking at the front door.”
    3. 2:15 pm, The smoke alarm. Adam always listens for smoke and CO alarms, day and night. Adam: “The smoke alarm is sounding in the kitchen.”
    4. 5:45 pm, A fall. Adam listens for the sounds that mean something is wrong, and gets the right person there on time. Adam: “I heard a fall in the living room. Gran is not answering.”
    5. 9:40 pm, June is awake. Maya and Dan are downstairs with the TV on. Adam hears June upstairs and tells them. Adam: “June is crying in the nursery.”
