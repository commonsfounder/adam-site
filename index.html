The problem01

AI agents can already book, buy and write for you, but only in the corners of life that run on software.

That is a small slice of people: the ones whose day already lives in flight portals, inboxes, Slack and code, where clean tools exist for an agent to use. For everyone else, the question stays the same: what can it actually do for me?

Most of life has no software built around it, and using an agent means starting every time. You open an app, put what is in your head into words, and go back and forth until it is right. Most people never think to, because you can’t want help you haven’t realised you could have.

And most of us spend most of our day at home, where almost nothing is built to help, or to make that time better: the everyday, the games, the music, the people. Nearly everything is aimed at work, or at being out.

What we make02

So we need a new family of devices, built to redefine how we play, create, and stay connected.

One speaker, two plugs and a phone app.

Adam is our first product, in development and not yet on sale. It hears what is going on in each room, with no cameras, and helps your family keep on top of it.

      What it already does04

      Tested on everyday household sounds.

      An agent can only act on what it notices at home, and sound is where we started. In noisy rooms, our sound model catches more than Apple’s own sound detection.

      We tested 15 everyday household sounds from FSD50K, a public research collection, on clips the model never trained on. Homes are noisy and echoey, so we added noise, echo and very short clips, and held both systems to the same strict limit of 1 false alarm in 100. Averaged over those hard conditions, ours catches about a quarter more real events than Apple’s, 54% against 43%. It has learned from public recordings only so far, and real homes are what will improve it.
